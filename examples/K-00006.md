HOLDS: when data goes missing, the loss is more often in the path than at the source — and the
path hides it, because a dropped record leaves a series that still looks like a series. Both
recorded cases were the plumbing: rotation wrote frames into a file nobody read again, and a
parser discarded a third of the frames while producing a smoother-looking curve.
LIMITS: this says where to look FIRST, not where the fault is. It is worth nothing if the source
is never checked at all, and it says nothing about a source that is genuinely dead — which
announces itself differently, as a series that stops rather than one that thins.
IMPLICATIONS: before changing anything at the source, count what was produced against what was
stored, at each hop. The count is cheap, it is a measurement rather than an inference, and in
both recorded cases it would have located the fault immediately.
